## Heimweg — Off-Roader Model

The first of the Models, the objects that travel between the Design Studies: an original electric luxury off-roader drawn as a minimal monolith. It stands 2.75 m tall and 2.52 m wide over flared arches, on 1.2 m tyres whose width you can choose. It has a glass canopy that narrows slightly toward the roof, a long bonnet, and a warm full-width light signature with a "welcome home" sweep. Working rear lights include tail, brake, sequential indicators, hazards and reverse.

Expedition kit: snorkel, winch, mud flaps, rock lights, a heated windscreen strip, a spare wheel and high-lift jack on the side-hinged tailgate, and an optional fuel can. Camp mode opens a side awning, a pop-up roof and a pull-out kitchen, and the car kneels when its doors open. Cameras include a see-through-bonnet ground view. Scenes: a studio, Bergruhe's drive, das Zuhause at night and the Atacama desert at Sternweh. It's also parked on Bergruhe's driveway, charges in Denkraum's and Geborgenheit's garages, and parks itself in Sternweh's showroom.

**Live:** https://24-00237.github.io/daszehause/heimweg/

## Denkraum — Studio Study

Fourth study: a quiet 43 m² studio for design and real estate work on the 38th floor of a typhoon-proof tower in Manila (or a mid-rise condo). Its signature is the vault: a back wall of locking narra drawers for deeds and drawings, a round steel vault door with a brass wheel (or a square one, or one hidden in the drawers), and a fire-rated vault room behind it with the server rack, a local AI and a NAS, so client data never leaves the room.

Five zones in one room: a model bench on the north glass, a meeting table with a presentation screen, a lounge, a reading nook in the glass corner and a nap corner with a linen curtain and a warm lamp. Box-in-box soundproofing and a hidden door from a private lift lobby. The tower stands on a paved plaza over one level of parking, where Heimweg charges by the lifts among the other cars. In the cutaway, the floors around and above the studio turn to X-ray instead of disappearing. Deep concrete fins, a sun ledge, planters and steel storm shutters for the heat, the monsoon and the typhoons. Scenes from early morning to city night, monsoon rain and a typhoon, with focus and nap light.

**Live:** https://24-00237.github.io/daszehause/denkraum/

## Geborgenheit — Sanctuary Study

Fifth study: a deep retreat set into the north wall of a river gorge in the Bukidnon highlands, Mindanao. Day to day it's a personal retreat; in an emergency it shelters the family for about a year on stored food, garden greens, river water and river power. You arrive in Heimweg, which parks and charges in a low pavilion on the plateau (a thin planted roof over narra slats, a stone wall to the gorge), then ride a glass lift down the gorge wall, like das Zuhause's glass core, and walk a lantern path along the water, with the lamps lighting one after another, to a carved stone and narra door whose greeting light comes on as you arrive.

At its heart is an underground garden under a light well cut up through the rock, with a thin waterfall down one wall, a glass lift up to a narra cabin at the rim (its roof is glass over the well; it has no deck and is closed to the gorge, and the lift runs only for the family), and the night sky framed overhead after dark. The vault sits beside the garden behind a round narra and bronze door (or a slab hidden in the memory wall), with art, books, essentials and a family archive with a drawer for each person. The rooms along the river open into one another and into the hall between stone piers, under 3.8 m ceilings, so the underground feels open rather than closed in. Rooms carved along the river: a river room with a long window over the river, a woven wall (T'nalak, Inaul or Dagmay) and a stone hearth; a family table cut from one slab; a reading nook, a sound room with a bronze horn that carries the river inside, a highland tea corner, a rain room, a cool cellar, a data room cooled by rock and river, and a family wing that stays sealed until shelter mode opens it: a small lounge with narra cabinets for supplies and two bedrooms, each with a wide low bed, bedside lamps, a narra slat headboard wall and a woven panel. Rain runs through visible channels to a cistern, the garden and back to the river; a hidden spiral stair, closed off in its own room behind a narra door, leads up to a hatch on the plateau. Off the lantern path, a gangway leads down to a floating teak dock where Fernweh's mahogany limousine tender is moored. On the plateau, a slatted fence on stone closes three sides of the compound (the cliff is the fourth), with a sliding gate for Heimweg and a side gate, both on palm readers; the gate slides open as you arrive, and the hidden hatch opens outside the gates. Panic mode, set off by river sensors at the weir or by hand, closes everything in order: a backflow valve on the overflow, steel roll-down shutters over every window and the door (lowest first), the stone slab behind the door, the gorge lift sent up and locked, and last a steel gate rising across the gorge path. Every shutter has a hand-crank release inside, and fire or bad air overrides it. Filtered air comes from a hidden intake and exhaust high on the plateau, and a steel rockfall canopy shelters the door. The floors sit 10 m above the river, 2 m clear of an 8 m surge like Sendong in 2011: the Flash flood scene raises the river 8 m, sets panic mode off and floats the dock up its piles while the water passes below the rooms. The glass lift has three stops (the rim, the door level and a river landing for the dock), and the river stop, dock and turbine are treated as expendable. Lighting is uplights and lamps only, with no ceiling lights anywhere. Scenes from highland dawn mist to a storm with the family sheltering.

## Sternweh — Observatory Home Study

Sixth study: a home under the stars and a pilgrimage, one great dome sunk into the Atacama Desert in Chile's coastal mountains near Paranal, so only its glass shows above the ground. You arrive only at night: Heimweg stops out in the desert and you walk the last stretch along low, shielded amber path lights to a straight stair cut down to a heavy bronze studded door; Heimweg then drives itself down a hidden ramp, under a sand-coloured lid, to its showroom.

Under one shallow lens of laminated, walkable glass (with a round panel over the court that slides open on still nights) are the three hearts: a sunken sky court with broad steps to sit or lie on, the sleeping chamber with the bed under the Milky Way, and a dark round star room with a bench round its wall and only a circle of sky above. Around them, under the same glass, are the open-sky bath, a guest room, a white studio a storey down, and Heimweg's showroom, with a simple RV parked alongside as a water reserve (it becomes Nebelfänger, a Model, later). Only the hallways have solid ceilings: a buried ring of classical panelled hallways with raised panels, wainscoting, deep arched door casings, a coffered ceiling, crown moulding, wide oak and woven runners. The kitchen sits below them, beside the white studio, and has the one spotlight, over its island. Lighting is lamps and floor uplights in the hallways and kitchen only; everywhere else the stars do the lighting, with tiny amber step markers. By day a woven alpaca textile draws under the glass to hold the sun's heat up against it, where vents in the rim let it out.

Off the grid: a solar field about 400 m out behind a low rise, fog catchers on the spurs toward the sea where the camanchaca comes in, an underground cistern with columns and arches like das Zuhause's, and the RV's reserve. The sky is drawn roughly as it really is from 24.6° south (the Milky Way's core overhead on winter nights, the Southern Cross, the Magellanic Clouds), with the month, the moon, the camanchaca and dust wind to choose. Views include sections east–west and north–south through the earth, a plan, lying down in the court to look up, and walk-throughs of every room and the cistern.

The glass is a steel grid with fine glass fins outside it, and it has a clear pane over the star room and another over the bed's pillow, so the sky there is unframed. The stair comes down in two flights with a landing, behind a low stone kerb, with drains for the rare flash flood; the ramp runs at 16% under a lid with an upstand. The showroom is behind fire-rated glass and a fire door. The eye adjusts as you move: exposure eases from the starlit rooms into the lamp-lit hallways, and a Lights setting can turn the house to amber only or off for the darkest sky.
